Two things to do before you write Draft 2

Thirty-five marks, and the biggest group is grammar — ten small joins and missing words, all quick repairs you can make while copying the story out. But her three longest margin notes are not about small words at all. They are about the pictures: what they show, and what your story does with them. Do these in order, because the ending cannot answer her question until the middle of the story is on the page.

Do first1

Follow the pictures — put Dad’s hands and the crash on the page

Three of her four long remarks ask the same thing: (Describe the picture: what was Dad doing?), (Not exactly! What action does the picture show?), and (What happened next? e.g. crashed into the tree and fell on my knees). The fourth is about order: Sequencing: he needs to alight the car first. Your story has the right events, but some are missing and some are in the wrong place.

The story as a list of beats — which are on your paper?
1 · ArriveCar stops, you get out. Your draft has the car and the zooming in the same breath, and she struck the car out. Put the arrival first.
2 · HandoverDad hands you the bicycle. On your paper already.
3 · HoldingDad supports your back. Not on your paper — she wrote it in: while Dad supported my back.
4 · Letting goDad slowly lets go. You wrote was gone; she wrote slowly let go of my back.
5 · The crashThe tree, and what it did to you. The only beat with no words at all. She gave an example you can use as it stands: crashed into the tree and fell on my knees.
6 · HospitalBack to Dad, then hospital. On your paper already.
The pair she has built

I started to cycle into the forest. … I panicked when Dad was gone

I started to cycle into the forest while Dad supported my back. … I panicked when Dad slowly let go of my back.

Both pieces are hers. Notice they echo: supported my back, let go of my back. The reader remembers the first hand and feels the second one go. Without the first, let go has nothing to push against.

And the gap in the middle

I could not control the wheels. I went back to Dad with a bruised leg.

I could not control the wheels. I crashed into the tree and fell on my knees. I went back to Dad with a bruised leg.

The bruised leg is the result. The crash is the cause. At the moment, the reader sees the cause as a gap and has to supply it from nowhere. Write one sentence for it and the whole middle of your story clicks.

The habit underneath

Whenever the picture has a second person, ask what their hands are doing. And before you leave a moment, ask what the picture shows next.

Do next2

Answer her question — did you learn to ride? Show it.

Her last remark: → Did he successfully learn how to cycle? Explain this. Your ending says you continued to work towards my goal and hoped to ride more often. It never says whether you can ride now. The reader has followed you through the crash and the hospital, and is left at the door. The answer is the best scene your story has left to give.

Three beats for the last paragraph
The try againBack on the bicycle. Where? With Dad? One sentence is enough — you already have found my bicycle and continued.
The proofOne moment where it works. Dad’s hands again — slowly let go — but this time you keep going. The echo from Recommendation 1 pays off here. This is what Explain this asks for: not I learnt, but how the reader can tell.
The line backKeep your closing line. Link to Topic is starred. Once you have shown the ride, from that day onwards has something real behind it.
Before and after

After that, my father and I found my bicycle and I continued to work towards my goal. From that day onwards, I hoped I would be able to ride a bicycle more often in the future.

After that, my father and I found my bicycle, and I continued to work towards my goal. Back at the park, Dad slowly let go of my back once more. This time I kept pedalling. The wheels wobbled, then steadied, and I rode all the way to the end of the path on my own. From that day onwards, I hoped I would be able to ride a bicycle more often in the future.

That is a shape, not your facts — only you know how your day really ended. If you did not manage to ride that day, the same shape still works: say what you could do by the end, even if it was three pedals. The reader just needs to see something happen.

Why second

This paragraph borrows from the first recommendation: the second pair of hands only means something if the first one is on the page. Do Recommendation 1 first, then write the ending.
